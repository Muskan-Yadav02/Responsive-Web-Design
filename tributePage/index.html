Marie Curie

The pioneering scientist who changed our understanding of radioactivity.

A remarkable scientific legacy

Marie Curie was a Polish-born physicist and chemist whose research transformed the scientific understanding of radioactivity. Alongside Pierre Curie, she investigated radioactive materials and helped discover the elements polonium and radium.

In 1903, Curie became the first woman to receive a Nobel Prize when she shared the Nobel Prize in Physics for her work on radiation. In 1911, she received a second Nobel Prize, this time in Chemistry, making her the first person to win Nobel Prizes in two different scientific fields.

During World War I, Curie helped develop mobile X-ray units that allowed doctors to examine wounded soldiers closer to the battlefield. Her dedication to science and humanitarian work left a lasting influence on medicine, physics, and generations of researchers.

“Nothing in life is to be feared; it is only to be understood.”
